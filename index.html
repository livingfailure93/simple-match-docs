Simple Match

Match-3 puzzles for Unity that are generated, solved exactly and rated.

Simple Match builds a match-3 puzzle from your settings, then proves it can be won and works out its par, the fewest moves it takes. It makes puzzles to clear, to collect, to peel layers off or to bring exit pieces down, with bonus pieces, crates, moss, countdowns, belts, portals and more, and refill levels where new pieces drop in from a seeded stream. Every puzzle is rated 1 to 5 stars and can be saved as a ScriptableObject, exported as a PNG or SVG picture, or played in the included 3D demo, which can also tell the player when a level can't be won any more.

What it is

Simple Match is a match-3 puzzle generator with a solver built in. The player swaps two pieces side by side to line up three or more of a kind; the line clears, the pieces above fall into the gap, and falling pieces can line up again (a cascade). You choose the board, the goal and what goes on it, and it builds a puzzle for you. Then it searches every position the puzzle can reach, so you know for certain that it can be won and how many moves the best win takes.

Everything is deterministic. The same settings and the same seed always give the same puzzle, and every puzzle keeps the settings and seed it was made from. Refill levels draw their new pieces from a seeded stream, so a level plays the same every time and can be proven too. When the generator can't give you exactly what you asked for, it says so in the puzzle's notes instead of quietly handing you something else.

In the editor pictures and in the demo every piece type has its own shape as well as its own colour (circle, square, triangle, diamond, hexagon and plus), and every gimmick has a shape of its own, so colour-blind players can tell them all apart.

The rules

Swap two neighbours

Swap two pieces that sit side by side (not diagonally). A swap is only allowed when it makes a line (or sets off a type clear or two bonus pieces together); otherwise it doesn't happen, and the rules say why.

Lines of three or more

Three or more of one type in a row or a column clear together. Lines that touch clear as one group, so an L, a T or a plus is one match.

Pieces fall

The pieces above an empty cell fall into it, toward row 0 (the bottom). Obstacles, crates and fixed pieces hold up what is above them. Gravity can be turned sideways or up cell by cell, and portals carry a falling piece elsewhere.

Cascades

Pieces that land in a line clear too, wave after wave, until nothing lines up. The move report records every wave.

Goals

Clear the board, collect a number of pieces of given shapes, clear every layer, or bring every exit piece down to an exit, within the move limit.

Won and lost

A move that meets the goal wins. A level is lost when the moves run out, a countdown reaches 0, or no swap is left.

The solver, the generator, the editor pictures and the demo all use the same rules (MatchRules), so they always agree. Every move returns a report: each wave's matches (type, size and shape), the bonus pieces made and fired, the gimmicks hit, the pieces dropped in.

Features

Proven par

The solver searches every position the puzzle can reach, so the par is the true minimum, not an estimate. It also counts the best wins and the different first moves that start one.

Difficulty in stars

1 to 5 stars from the exact chance that a player swapping at random wins (refill levels: seeded half-greedy players), measured so that each star covers about a fifth of generated puzzles. Ask for a star count and the generator keeps trying until it gets one.

Boards

3 to 12 cells each way, 2 to 6 piece types, square or shaped (diamond, heart, ring or drawn by you), with obstacles if you like.

Every gimmick matters

A crate, fixed piece, filter, belt or countdown is kept only when the puzzle really uses it, and a bonus puzzle can be asked to need its bonus pieces.

Can this still be won?

MatchSolver.Outlook answers it for any position in play: won, can win (in how many moves, along which line), or can't (and why). It is the hint, auto-solve and the dead-end warning in the demo.

27 measured presets

From a first puzzle to expert, shapes, every gimmick and refill levels, each measured on 24 seeds. Auto fixes settings that fight each other and says what it changed.

Pictures

Save any puzzle as a PNG or SVG: the colour view, a black and white print view, or an answer key with every swap numbered, at any step of the solution, with a goal line and a legend.

ScriptableObjects

Save one puzzle as an asset or a whole set as a pack; they load back exactly as they were saved.

A 3D demo

Built in code: four modes, Endless puzzles made to order, four level packs (48 levels), undo, hint, auto-solve, sounds and effects, in Built-in, URP and HDRP.

Why "verified"

  • The par is real. The solver searches every position, so a 7-move puzzle really takes 7 moves. A search cut short is marked as not proven, and the generator never accepts one.
  • It can be won. Puzzle-mode boards are built backwards from the goal, so they always have a win, and the solver proves it. Refill levels are proven over their own seeded stream.
  • Stars from measurement. The bands were set over hundreds of generated puzzles of each kind (293 everyday, 234 with bonus pieces, 112 refill levels), so a star means the same on every puzzle, whatever its goal or gimmicks.
  • Misses are reported. When a star count, a par window or a gimmick can't be met, the puzzle's notes say what happened and what you got instead.
  • Checked. Over 250 headless tests on the rules, the solver and the generator, with break passes that plant bugs in the code and confirm the tests catch them, and checks in real Unity of the editor tools and the demo.

Getting started

1. Open the generator

Window > Living Failure > Simple Match > Match Generator.

2. Pick a preset and generate

Press Presets, pick one (for example Gimmicks/Breakables) and press Generate.

3. Save it

Save a puzzle as an asset, or the whole set as a pack, and play it with your own game or the demo.

4. Play the demo

Open Assets/SimpleMatch/Scenes/SimpleMatchDemo.unity and press Play.

The Quick Start goes through it step by step, with the code to generate and play a puzzle yourself.
