Simple Match

Match-3 puzzles for Unity that are generated, solved exactly and rated.

Simple Match builds a match-3 puzzle from your settings, then proves it can be won and works out its par, the fewest moves it takes. It makes puzzles to clear, to collect, to peel layers off or to bring exit pieces down, with bonus pieces, crates, moss, countdowns, belts, portals and more, and refill levels where new pieces drop in from a seeded stream. Every puzzle is rated 1 to 5 stars and can be saved as a ScriptableObject, exported as a PNG or SVG picture, or played in the included 3D demo, which can also tell the player when a level can't be won any more.

Version 1.0, by Living Failure

What it is

Simple Match is a match-3 puzzle generator with a solver built in. The player swaps two pieces side by side to line up three or more of a kind; the line clears, the pieces above fall into the gap, and falling pieces can line up again (a cascade). You choose the board, the goal and what goes on it, and it builds a puzzle for you. Then it searches every position the puzzle can reach, so you know for certain that it can be won and how many moves the best win takes.

Everything is deterministic. The same settings and the same seed always give the same puzzle, and every puzzle keeps the settings and seed it was made from. Refill levels draw their new pieces from a seeded stream, so a level plays the same every time and can be proven too. When the generator can't give you exactly what you asked for, it says so in the puzzle's notes instead of quietly handing you something else.

In the editor pictures and in the demo every piece type has its own shape as well as its own colour (circle, square, triangle, diamond, hexagon and plus), and every gimmick has a shape of its own, so colour-blind players can tell them all apart.

The rules

Swap two neighbours

Swap two pieces that sit side by side (not diagonally). A swap is only allowed when it makes a line (or sets off a type clear or two bonus pieces together); otherwise it doesn't happen, and the rules say why.

Lines of three or more

Three or more of one type in a row or a column clear together. Lines that touch clear as one group, so an L, a T or a plus is one match.

Pieces fall

The pieces above an empty cell fall into it, toward row 0 (the bottom). Obstacles, crates and fixed pieces hold up what is above them. Gravity can be turned sideways or up cell by cell, and portals carry a falling piece elsewhere.

Cascades

Pieces that land in a line clear too, wave after wave, until nothing lines up. The move report records every wave.

Goals

Clear the board, collect a number of pieces of given shapes, clear every layer, or bring every exit piece down to an exit, within the move limit.

Won and lost

A move that meets the goal wins. A level is lost when the moves run out, a countdown reaches 0, or no swap is left.

The solver, the generator, the editor pictures and the demo all use the same rules (MatchRules), so they always agree. Every move returns a report: each wave's matches (type, size and shape), the bonus pieces made and fired, the gimmicks hit, the pieces dropped in.

Features

Proven par

The solver searches every position the puzzle can reach, so the par is the true minimum, not an estimate. It also counts the best wins and the different first moves that start one.

Difficulty in stars

1 to 5 stars from the exact chance that a player swapping at random wins (refill levels: seeded half-greedy players), measured so that each star covers about a fifth of generated puzzles. Ask for a star count and the generator keeps trying until it gets one.

Boards

3 to 12 cells each way, 2 to 6 piece types, square or shaped (diamond, heart, ring or drawn by you), with obstacles if you like.

Every gimmick matters

A crate, fixed piece, filter, belt or countdown is kept only when the puzzle really uses it, and a bonus puzzle can be asked to need its bonus pieces.

Can this still be won?

MatchSolver.Outlook answers it for any position in play: won, can win (in how many moves, along which line), or can't (and why). It is the hint, auto-solve and the dead-end warning in the demo.

27 measured presets

From a first puzzle to expert, shapes, every gimmick and refill levels, each measured on 24 seeds. Auto fixes settings that fight each other and says what it changed.

Pictures

Save any puzzle as a PNG or SVG: the colour view, a black and white print view, or an answer key with every swap numbered, at any step of the solution, with a goal line and a legend.

ScriptableObjects

Save one puzzle as an asset or a whole set as a pack; they load back exactly as they were saved.

A 3D demo

Built in code: four modes, Endless puzzles made to order, four level packs (48 levels), undo, hint, auto-solve, sounds and effects, in Built-in, URP and HDRP.

Why "verified"

  • The par is real. The solver searches every position, so a 7-move puzzle really takes 7 moves. A search cut short is marked as not proven, and the generator never accepts one.
  • It can be won. Puzzle-mode boards are built backwards from the goal, so they always have a win, and the solver proves it. Refill levels are proven over their own seeded stream.
  • Stars from measurement. The bands were set over hundreds of generated puzzles of each kind (293 everyday, 234 with bonus pieces, 112 refill levels), so a star means the same on every puzzle, whatever its goal or gimmicks.
  • Misses are reported. When a star count, a par window or a gimmick can't be met, the puzzle's notes say what happened and what you got instead.
  • Checked. Over 250 headless tests on the rules, the solver and the generator, with break passes that plant bugs in the code and confirm the tests catch them, and checks in real Unity of the editor tools and the demo.

Quick Start

From the window to a playable level in a few minutes.

Five minutes from opening the window to a saved, proven puzzle, and the code to make and play one yourself.

1. The generator window

Open Window > Living Failure > Simple Match > Match Generator. The settings are on the left, and the puzzles you generate appear on the right, each with its picture, its par, its stars and anything the generator wants you to know. Warnings under the settings say when two of them fight each other.

2. Generate a puzzle

Press Presets and pick one, for example Gimmicks/Breakables (a 7 x 7 board with crates to break). Press Generate. The puzzle is built and solved on a background thread, with a progress bar, Cancel, and a time limit (Time limit (s)) so a hard request can never hang the editor.

Not sure two settings go together? Press Auto: it keeps what you asked for and changes whatever fights it, and tells you what it changed and why.

Set Batch count above 1 to make several at once; Seed makes a puzzle you can make again (the same settings and seed always give the same puzzle), and Random picks a fresh one.

3. Read the result

Select a puzzle on the right to see it large. The pane under the picture says, in words:

  • the goal ("clear the board in 6 moves"), the board and its gimmicks;
  • par, the proven fewest moves, and the move limit (par plus Moves to spare);
  • stars, 1 to 5, and how many best wins there are;
  • every move of the solution, step by step (Show swaps, the arrows), with what each move did;
  • notes: anything that couldn't be met exactly ("asked 4 stars, got 3") and what you got instead.

Hover a cell for what is on it. Copy seed and Copy solution copy them as text.

4. Save it

Press Save:

  • Current saves the selected puzzle as a MatchPuzzleAsset;
  • Pack (all) saves the whole set as a MatchPuzzlePack, or Pack (on target only) just the ones that met every request;
  • Pictures of the set writes a PNG or SVG of each puzzle and its answer key.

Select a saved asset to see the same picture and pane in its Inspector, with Verify (re-solve it and check nothing changed) and Re-roll (a new seed, the same settings).

5. From code

Everything the window does is one call away. Make a puzzle from a preset (or your own MatchSettings):

using SimpleMatch;
using UnityEngine;

var settings = MatchPresets.Find("Gimmicks/Breakables").Make();
settings.Seed = "level-1";                       // the same seed, the same puzzle
MatchData puzzle = MatchGenerator.GenerateVerified(settings);

Debug.Log($"{MatchNarration.GoalSentence(puzzle)}: par {puzzle.Par}, {puzzle.Difficulty} stars");
foreach (var note in puzzle.Notes) Debug.Log(note);   // anything not met exactly

A saved asset gives you the same MatchData:

public MatchPuzzleAsset level;      // assign in the Inspector
MatchData puzzle = level.Puzzle;
MatchData third = myPack.Get(2);    // a MatchPuzzlePack

6. Play it

The rules work on a MatchBoard (what never changes) and a MatchState (the position). Each move returns a report of everything it did, wave by wave:

MatchBoard board = puzzle.Board();
MatchState state = puzzle.Start();
int moves = 0;

// a swap of two neighbouring cells (cell = y * Width + x, row 0 at the bottom)
var (a, b) = puzzle.SolutionMoves()[0];
if (MatchRules.TrySwap(board, state, a, b, out MoveReport report, out string why))
{
    moves++;
    Debug.Log($"cleared {report.Cleared} pieces in {report.Steps.Count} waves");
}
else Debug.Log("refused: " + why);           // "the swap makes no match", ...

// can it still be won from here, and how?
MatchOutlook outlook = MatchSolver.Outlook(puzzle, state, moves);
if (outlook.Kind == OutlookKind.CanWin) Debug.Log($"{outlook.MovesNeeded} more, hint {outlook.Hint}");
if (outlook.Kind == OutlookKind.CannotWin) Debug.Log("no way to win: " + outlook.Why);

Every function is described in the API Reference; how the settings shape a puzzle is in Generating.

7. The demo

Open Assets/SimpleMatch/Scenes/SimpleMatchDemo.unity and press Play. Pick a mode, then Endless (a new puzzle made to order) or a level of its pack. Tap a piece and then a neighbour (or drag it) to swap. U undoes, R restarts, H shows a hint, F solves. The Demos page shows how it is built, so you can take what you need.

Generating

Every setting, what it does, and what was measured.

Every setting below is a field of MatchSettings and a control in the Match Generator window. Where a setting says "measured", the number comes from the harness that tests the asset, over hundreds of generated puzzles.

How a puzzle is made

A puzzle-mode board without bonus pieces is built backwards: starting from an empty board, the builder adds lines of three, one at a time, each one undoing a swap, so the board always has a win and the builder knows one. The solver then searches every position to prove the true par. A board with bonus pieces is dealt instead (every cell gets a piece, no line at the start), and kept only when the solver proves the goal can be reached. A refill level is dealt too, with new pieces drawn from a seeded stream, and proven over that stream.

Then the gimmicks you asked for are placed where the known win still works, each one checked, the move limit is set, and the stars are measured. MatchGenerator.GenerateVerified tries more seeds until every target is met, or returns the closest puzzle with a note.

The board

SettingWhat it does
Width, Height3 to 12 cells each way: the most room the puzzle may use.
OutlineFull (the rectangle), Cross, Ring, Diamond, Circle, Heart, L, T, U, or Custom (CustomShape: rows of text, top row first, '#' a cell). Cells outside the outline are a floor the pieces rest on.
Types2 to 6 piece types (each its own shape and colour).
LinesHow many lines of three a built puzzle is made from (2 to 15): it holds 3 x Lines pieces, and Lines is the most moves a win needs. Measured: up to 12 lines the par is proven in under 0.3 s, 13 to 15 take 1 to 10 s. The par comes out below the lines (on 1,440 built puzzles it was at most the lines minus 1).
ObstaclesThe share of cells scattered as obstacles (0 to 0.3). After the build, an obstacle with nothing resting on it is removed, so a puzzle keeps a third to a half of them. Measured: up to 0.2 every board builds and puzzles get harder; at 0.3 a fifth to two fifths of builds fail.
FitCut a full board down to the cells the puzzle uses (empty side columns, the rows above the tallest stack). Nothing is lost: without refills no piece ever reaches them.

Goals and the move limit

GoalWhat the player must do
ClearAll (the default)Clear every piece (and every crate).
CollectClear a number of pieces of one type (CollectType, -1 = the generator picks the one that takes the most moves; CollectCount, 0 = every one). Measured: about half the moves of clearing the board, but wins can run 2 to 5 moves longer than par, so the move limit is what makes it a puzzle.
ClearLayersTake every layer off the layered cells (pieces may be left). See Layers below.
ExitAllBring every exit piece down to an exit (pieces may be left).

MoveSlack (0 to 5) is the moves allowed beyond par: the move limit is par plus it. On clear-all puzzles almost every win takes exactly par moves, so it barely matters there; on collect puzzles it decides the difficulty. Refill levels use RefillMoveSlack (default 2).

Bonus pieces

With Bonuses on, four in a line leaves a Line clear, an L, T or plus a Pattern clear, five in a line a Type clear; two swapped together make a combo. A bonus puzzle is dealt, not built, so Lines and Fit don't apply. Measured: with bonuses, 75 to 100% of full boards from 6 x 6 up can be cleared, while plain lines clear 0 to 3% of them.

  • NeedBonuses (on by default, clear all): keep only boards that plain lines alone can't clear, so the player has to make bonus pieces and use them.
  • Pattern: what a Pattern clear hits, painted on a 7 x 7 grid (MatchPattern: Square, the default 3 x 3, Plus, Diagonal, Diamond, Big, or MatchPattern.FromRows).
  • Time (measured, proof and exact odds included): 6 x 6 boards 0.1 to 1.3 s, 7 x 7 with 5 types 1 to 3 s, 7 x 7 with 4 types about 14 s; bigger boards mostly can't be proven.

Gimmicks

Each gimmick is placed only where the win the generator knows still works, and kept only when it changes the puzzle, so every one matters. Placing them at random instead leaves most boards unsolvable (measured below).

SettingWhat it adds, and what was measured
LayerShare, MaxLayers (goal ClearLayers)Layers on a share of the cells a known clear clears (0.05 to 1), 1 to 3 each. Every puzzle builds in milliseconds; from 0.6 up random players win half as often or less.
Breakables, MaxHitsCrates on cells that stay empty for good, next to where the known swaps make matches; 1 to 3 hits each. Every such puzzle can be cleared, par rises a little and random players win up to half as often. (Slid under columns instead, only 35 to 68% can be cleared.)
FixedPiecesA share of the safe pieces fixed (safe: the known win plays out the same with it pinned). Every puzzle stays solvable (random pieces: 0 to 38%); par rises by 0.2 to 0.8, but a random player wins a little more often (fewer pieces to swap wrongly), so stars can drop.
FiltersA share of the safe cells filtered to their piece's type. Every puzzle stays solvable (random cells: 0 to 14%); par rises by 0.1 to 0.4.
SpreadingUp to 8 moss cells (clear all, bonuses off), each kept only when the known swaps still clear the board. A puzzle keeps 3 to 4 at most. A random player wins 2 to 7 times less often: moss makes puzzles harder, since every move that leaves it alone feeds it.
Countdowns, CountdownSlackUp to 8 countdown pieces, each given the move the known win clears it on, plus 0 to 3 moves of slack. With no slack they make puzzles much harder (random players win 2 to 20 times less often); one move of slack mostly takes that away.
ExitPiecesUp to 4 exit pieces on top of stacks, with an exit at the stack's floor (built puzzles). With ExitAll the par is 3 to 5.
BeltsUp to 3 conveyor belts (a row that wraps, or a small rectangle's ring): 8 tried per belt, each proven, the hardest kept. Random players win 3 to 12 times less often.

Portals, gravity and spawners are in the rules, the solver, the pictures and the demo, but the generator doesn't place them: measured, portals mostly made shortcuts and gravity changed little. Set them on a puzzle yourself (MatchData.Portal, Gravity, Spawner) and the solver proves it as usual.

Refill levels

With Refill on, the board is dealt full and new pieces drop in from the top of each fall path after every fall, from a seeded stream, so a level plays the same every time. When no swap is left the board reshuffles (the same pieces, no line, a swap guaranteed). Goals: Collect (0 = a third of the board's cells), ClearLayers, or ExitAll with ExitDrops exit pieces dropping in (1 to 8, at most 2 on the board at once). Clear all can't be met with refills and becomes Collect. Bonus pieces on or off. Crates, fixed pieces, filters, moss, countdowns, exit pieces and belts are switched off on refill levels.

Par is proven over the stream; the stars come from how often 400 seeded half-greedy players win within the move limit (par plus RefillMoveSlack, default 2: more moves to spare make a level easier, and its stars say so). Measured: par is proven on almost every level up to 8 x 8 with 5 types, in up to about 7 s; a large or hard level can take 10 to 30 s.

Stars

1 to 5 stars, each covering about a fifth of generated puzzles of its kind. There are three scales:

ScaleFrom1 star ... 5 stars
Everyday (bonuses off)the exact chance a player swapping at random wins within the move limit (measured over 293 puzzles)1: wins half the time or more; 2: 20%+; 3: 6%+; 4: 1.4%+; 5: less
Bonus (bonuses on)the same chance (a random player almost never clears a full board, so it has its own bands; 234 puzzles)1: 0.07%+; 2: 0.02%+; 3: 0.005%+; 4: 0.0004%+; 5: less
Refill400 seeded half-greedy players (112 refill levels, at par + 2)1: they win 70%+; 2: 30%+; 3: 9%+; 4: 1.3%+; 5: less

One scale per side, whatever the goal or gimmick, so a star means the same on every puzzle. The chance ranks puzzles the way a careful player does (rank agreement 0.95, measured), while par alone ranks them poorly (0.68).

Targets and the search

SettingWhat it does
TargetDifficulty1 to 5 stars (0 = any): more seeds are tried until one rates that.
MinPar, MaxParA par window (MaxPar 0 = no limit).

In the window, Time limit (s) stops a generation that runs too long (it throws a TimeoutException naming the limit), and Cancel stops it at once; from code, pass a CancellationToken and a limit to GenerateVerified.

Presets and Auto

27 presets in the window's Presets menu (MatchPresets.All), each measured on 24 seeds, with its par and stars in its description:

  • Puzzles: First puzzle (5 x 5, 3 shapes), Small, Medium, Large (9 x 9, 5 shapes).
  • Difficulty: Easy, Medium, Hard, Expert (aiming for 2 to 5 stars).
  • Shapes: Diamond, Heart, Ring, Obstacles.
  • Goals: Collect.
  • Bonus pieces: Clear with bonuses, Collect.
  • Gimmicks: Layers, Breakables, Fixed pieces, Type filters, Spreading, Countdowns, Exit pieces, Conveyor belts.
  • Refill: Collect, Collect with bonuses, Layers, Exit drops.

Auto (MatchPresets.Tune) returns a copy of your settings with whatever fights itself fixed, and a sentence per change. It keeps the gimmick you asked for and changes what can't live with it: refill levels turn clear all into collect and switch off the gimmicks they don't have; too many lines for the board, a star or par target the board can't reach, a custom outline that doesn't parse. Without Auto the builder does the same, drops what doesn't fit, and says so in the puzzle's notes.

When a request can't be met

The generator never quietly hands you something else. MatchData.Notes lists anything it couldn't do exactly, for example "No puzzle met the settings in 20 tries (... difficulty 5 ...): this is the closest", or "Breakables apply to clear-all puzzles (breaking them is part of clearing the board): this puzzle has none.", and MatchAudit.MeetsRequest says whether a puzzle met every request (MatchAudit.Missing lists what it missed). In the window, the Save menu can save only the puzzles on target. MatchAudit.Verify re-solves any puzzle and says whether its stored verdict still holds.

The rules the generator and the solver use, so a game built on them always agrees with the par.

MemberWhat it does

MatchState state = m.Start();
int[] mostLeft = m.Goal.MostLeft(board, m.Start()), counts = new int[board.Types + 1];
int moves = 0;

bool Play(int a, int b)
{
    if (!MatchRules.TrySwap(board, state, a, b, out MoveReport report, out string why)) { Debug.Log(why); return false; }
    moves++;
    if (MatchSolver.Met(board, state, m.Goal, mostLeft, counts)) Debug.Log("won");
    else if (state.Exploded || moves >= m.MoveLimit) Debug.Log("lost");
    return true;
}

Moves, waves and beats

A MoveReport says what a move did. Steps holds one CascadeStep per wave: the groups matched, the cells cleared, the bonus pieces made (Made) and fired (Fired), the layers, crates and moss hit, the exit pieces that left, the new pieces dropped in. The report also has Cleared, Combo, CascadeDepth, SpreadTo, Exploded and Reshuffled.

Set Trace before the move and Beats gets every step in order, each a MoveBeat with its Kind (Swap, Clear, Fall, Exit, Drop, Belt, Spread, Tick, Reshuffle), the pieces it moved (Moves: from, to), the cells it is about, and a copy of the position after it (After). That is what a game animates; the demo's MatchGame plays them one by one.

var report = new MoveReport { Trace = true };
if (MatchRules.Refusal(board, state, a, b) == null)
{
    MatchRules.ApplySwap(board, state, a, b, report);
    foreach (MoveBeat beat in report.Beats)
        Debug.Log($"{beat.Kind}: {beat.Moves.Count} pieces moved, {beat.Cells.Count} cells");
}

MatchSolver.Outlook(m, state, movesMade, maxStates = 200000, board = null) answers it for any position in play, puzzle mode and refill levels alike. A MatchOutlook:

A puzzle by hand

Fill a MatchData, check it with MatchRules.Validate, and MatchAudit.Verify solves it and stamps its par, odds and stars, as the generator would.

// 4 x 2, two types: swapping the third column's pieces makes two lines of four
var hand = new MatchData
{
    Width = 4, Height = 2, Types = 2,
    Cells = new sbyte[] { 0, 0, 1, 0,     // row 0 (the bottom)
                          1, 1, 0, 1 },   // row 1
    Goal = MatchGoal.ClearAll(),
};
if (!MatchRules.Validate(hand, out string error)) Debug.Log(error);
else
{
    MatchAudit.Verify(hand, out string before, out string after);
    Debug.Log(after);   // "4 x 2, clear all, par 1 in 1 moves, 2 stars"
}

Gimmicks are arrays of the board's size (for example hand.Fixed = new bool[8], then hand.Fixed[1] = true). The generator doesn't place portals, gravity or spawners, but the rules, the solver, the pictures and the demo all play them, so set them by hand like this.

Demos

A complete match-3 game built on the asset, made entirely in code.

The demo is a small, complete match-3 game built on the asset, so you can play what the generator makes and take whatever you need from it. Everything in it is made in code: no prefabs, models, textures or audio files.

The demo scene

Open Assets/SimpleMatch/Scenes/SimpleMatchDemo.unity and press Play. The scene holds a camera, a light and one MatchDemo component with the four level packs in its slots. It needs the Input System package (Active Input Handling set to Input System Package or Both) and Unity UI.

One component: MatchDemo

Drop MatchDemo in any scene with a camera and press Play. It builds the board and the game, the HUD, the hub, the sounds and the effects, and lights the scene if it has no light. Its fields:

FieldWhat it does
CameraThe camera that shows the board (empty = the main camera).
UiThroughCameraDraw the HUD and the hub through the camera (Screen Space - Camera) instead of over the screen.
PitchHow steeply the camera looks down to start with.
HdrpLookHDRP only: a small volume (fixed exposure, a gradient sky) so it looks as it does in the other pipelines.
ClearPack, BonusPack, GimmicksPack, RefillPackThe level packs (MatchPuzzlePack) of the four modes; empty = that mode's Level Pack tab says so.

The flow

A welcome panel says what Simple Match is; Let's play goes on to four mode cards, each with a picture of a real puzzle of its mode. A card opens its setup:

  • Endless: pick the kind and the difficulty (1 to 5 stars) and press Play. A brand new puzzle is made to order on a background thread (on WebGL, which has no threads, straight away). New puzzle on the win panel makes another to the same order.
  • Level Pack: the mode's levels as numbered buttons, grey to do, the mode's colour solved, gold solved on par. Play starts the first level not yet solved. The best moves per level are kept (in PlayerPrefs: MatchPackProgress), and the win panel says "Level complete!" or how you did against your best. Next level goes on.

Menu or Esc goes back. The hub's Warnings button (or W in play) turns the dead-end warning on or off, remembered between sessions.

The four modes

ModeKinds (Endless)
ClearSquare or shaped (a diamond, heart or ring) boards: clear every piece.
Bonus PiecesClear the board or collect, with bonus pieces (collect goes up to 4 stars).
GimmicksLayers, crates, fixed pieces, filters, moss, countdowns, exits or belts.
RefillClassic match-3 with new pieces dropping in: collect, clear layers, or bring down the exit pieces that drop in.

The recipes behind each kind and star count are in MatchEndless.Settings, pure C#, and were measured on 8 deals each: 72 of 74 land on their stars every time, and the other two (refill exit drops at 3 and 4 stars) land one star off in one deal of eight. Most puzzles are made in under 3 seconds; the slowest, refill exit drops and layers at 4 stars, can take 10 to 30.

The level packs

Four packs in Assets/SimpleMatch/Generated, 48 levels in all: Clear 12, Bonus Pieces 10, Gimmicks 16 (each gimmick twice, gently and then hard) and Refill 10. Each climbs from 1 star to 5, so nobody starts on the hard ones. They were made from fixed seeds with the same Endless recipes, and every level was checked: valid, re-verified unchanged, on its exact stars, everything asked for in it, and played through the game to par.

Make your own in the Match Generator window (Save > Pack (all)) and drop it in a pack slot.

Playing

Tap a piece (it lifts over a white ring) and then a neighbour, or drag a piece onto a neighbour, to swap. A swap that makes no match slides over and back; a piece that can't move wiggles. Every step of a move is shown: the swap, the matches popping, pieces falling along their real paths (sideways with gravity, through portals), new pieces sliding in, belts moving, a reshuffle lifting every moved piece to its own height so none passes through another. After each step the board is put exactly where the rules say, so it never drifts.

The HUD shows the goal in words, how far along it is, the board, the difficulty, moves against the limit, and a status line for a win, a loss, a refused swap's reason, or the dead-end warning ("This can't be won any more: it needs more than the 3 moves left. Undo or restart."). That warning comes from asking MatchSolver.Outlook after every move, on a background thread, so play never waits for it.

Sounds and effects

MatchBeeper synthesizes every sound in code: a pop with a note for each match, one step up the chord for each wave of a cascade, a sparkle for a bonus piece made, a blast for each kind of bonus clear, a knock or a crack for a crate, a tick-tock for a countdown on its last move, and tunes for the win and the loss. MatchEffects throws small lit cubes for each moment (no particle shaders, so they look the same in every pipeline): bursts in a piece's colour, a streak along a line clear, splinters, confetti. Both just listen to the game's Cue and Moment events.

Render pipelines

The demo makes its materials from the active pipeline's default lit shader, so it works in URP, HDRP and the Built-in pipeline (checked in all three: every material on the pipeline's lit shader, none pink, the pictures the same). A shader that only code uses would be stripped from a build, so MatchLookKeeper writes Runtime/Demo/Resources/SimpleMatchLook.mat before every build, a plain material with that shader, so the build keeps it. It changes no project settings, and you can delete the material: the next build writes it again. On HDRP, DemoHdrp adds a small volume so it looks the same.

Troubleshooting

What the notes mean, and what to do when something isn't as expected.

Reading the notes

Whenever the generator can't give you exactly what you asked for, it writes a note on the puzzle instead of quietly giving you less. The analysis pane shows every note, and so does MatchData.Notes from code. MatchAudit.MeetsRequest(puzzle) tells you whether a puzzle got everything its settings asked for and MatchAudit.Missing(puzzle) lists what it missed. In the window, Only on target hides the puzzles that missed something, and Save > Pack (on target only) leaves them out of a pack.

"No puzzle met the settings in 20 tries (...): this is the closest"

No seed gave a puzzle that met every hard requirement: solvable, the par proven and inside your par window, on your star target, with the bonus pieces needed if you asked for that. The note lists what it was looking for. Try, in this order:

  • Auto, which fixes settings that fight each other and says what it changed;
  • more Max attempts under Advanced, so more seeds are tried;
  • a wider par window, or a board that can hold the par you want (a built puzzle's par is at most its Lines minus 1);
  • one star more or less (see below).

The stars aren't the ones asked for

The stars come from how often a player swapping at random (on refill levels, a half-greedy player) wins within the move limit. Some settings can't reach every star:

  • Small boards make easy puzzles. Measured on the presets: 5 x 5 with 3 shapes rates 1 to 3 stars (mostly 2), 6 x 6 with 4 shapes 1 to 4, and 7 x 7 with 4 shapes mostly 4 to 5. For 4 or 5 stars use a bigger board, more lines or more shapes, or a gimmick that makes puzzles harder (spreading, countdowns with no slack, belts).
  • Moves to spare make a puzzle easier, so its stars drop: on collect goals and refill levels this setting decides much of the difficulty.
  • Collect on bonus boards is easy once bonus pieces fly: it rates up to about 4 stars.
  • Fixed pieces and type filters raise the par but can make a random player win slightly more often (fewer pieces to swap wrongly), so they can lower the stars.

Fewer gimmicks than asked

Every gimmick is placed only where the known win still works, and kept only when it changes the puzzle. When there is no such place, the puzzle gets fewer (or none) and a note says why, for example "Breakables: no cell that stays empty is next to a match of this puzzle's swaps, so it has none. A taller board leaves more room above the pieces". What helps:

  • Crates, moss, exit pieces need empty room above the pieces: a taller board, fewer lines, or Fit off. They are for clear-all puzzles without bonus pieces (exit pieces also for ExitAll); a dealt bonus board is full, so it has none.
  • Spreading keeps 3 to 4 cells at most; GenerateVerified tries more seeds when a puzzle got none.
  • Refill levels switch off crates, fixed pieces, filters, moss, countdowns, exit pieces and belts.

"The solver's state budget ran out"

The solver searched as many positions as State budget (under Advanced) allows without finishing. The generator never accepts such a puzzle as proven; it tries another seed. Raise the budget, or make the puzzle smaller: fewer lines (up to 12 are proven in under 0.3 s), fewer shapes on a bonus board, or a smaller board.

Generation feels slow

Measured times for one puzzle, proof and odds included: built puzzles up to 12 lines well under a second, 13 to 15 lines 1 to 10 s; bonus boards 6 x 6 up to about 1 s, 7 x 7 with 5 shapes 1 to 3 s, 7 x 7 with 4 shapes about 14 s; refill levels mostly a few seconds, large or hard ones 10 to 30 s. A star target multiplies that by the seeds it takes to hit.

Generation runs on a background thread, so the editor stays usable. Set Time limit (s) and a puzzle that takes longer is listed as not made, and Cancel stops at once. For bonus boards, 5 shapes are faster than 4 (more shapes, fewer matches to search).

The warning says nothing on a refill level

The dead-end warning asks MatchSolver.Outlook, which searches up to MatchGame.OutlookBudget positions (200,000), then once more ten times as far. On a big refill level it can still run out and answer Unknown, and the demo then says nothing rather than guess. It is never wrong, only sometimes silent. Raise the budget for more answers at the cost of more time on a worker thread.

A hand-made board is refused

MatchRules.Validate(puzzle, out error) says why in words. The usual causes:

  • a piece floating over an empty cell (every piece must rest on the bottom, a blocked cell, a crate or another piece);
  • a line of three already on the board at the start;
  • an array of the wrong length (each gimmick array has one entry per cell), or a type outside 0 to Types - 1;
  • row 0 is the bottom row: a board typed top row first comes out upside down.

Then MatchAudit.Verify solves it and stamps its par, odds and stars.

The demo ignores taps or keys

  • The demo reads the Input System package: set Project Settings > Player > Active Input Handling to Input System Package or Both.
  • The HUD and hub make an EventSystem with the Input System's UI module when the scene has none. If your scene has one with the old StandaloneInputModule, swap it for InputSystemUIInputModule.
  • Taps are ignored while a move is still playing out and over the HUD's buttons.

Pink or dark in a pipeline

  • Pink in a build: the demo makes its materials in code, and a build strips shaders that only code uses. Before every build, MatchLookKeeper writes Runtime/Demo/Resources/SimpleMatchLook.mat with the active pipeline's lit shader, so the build keeps it. If you see pink, check that file exists after a build (deleting it is fine: the next build writes it again).
  • Dark or tinted in HDRP: out of the box HDRP uses physical light units, automatic exposure and sky lighting. MatchDemo.HdrpLook (on by default) adds a global volume at priority 10 with a fixed exposure and a gradient sky, and sets the sun in lux, so the demo looks the same as on the other pipelines. Turn it off to let your own volumes and lights take over.

Resetting the demo's progress

The demo saves each pack level's best moves in PlayerPrefs, under SimpleMatch.Demo.Best.<pack name>.<level>, and the warning toggle under SimpleMatch.Demo.WarnDeadEnds. Call MatchPackProgress.Clear(packName, count) to forget a pack, or delete those keys. A pack's progress is filed under its pack name, so renaming a pack starts it fresh.

Seeds and versions

The same settings and seed always give the same puzzle within a version of Simple Match. A future version that improves the generator may change what a seed produces. Saved assets store the puzzle itself, not just its seed, so your saved puzzles and packs never change. Verified generation stores the seed that actually worked, such as level-1#2, so copying a puzzle's own seed always rebuilds that exact puzzle. Refill levels store their stream's seed too, so they drop the same pieces every time.

Console messages

  • "[Simple Match] Documentation page not found": the documentation menu looks for these pages in a folder whose path contains SimpleMatch and Documentation. Keep the Documentation folder inside the Simple Match folder.
  • "[Simple Match] Generation failed": something went wrong on the window's generation thread. Please send the message with your settings and seed.
  • "[Simple Match] Kept ... in the build for the demo": the build step wrote the look material, which is expected after you switch render pipelines.
  • "[Simple Match] No lit shader found for the active render pipeline": the pipeline asset has no default material, so the demo may draw pink in a build. Give the pipeline asset a default material.
  • "[Simple Match] Exported 12 files from ...": a pack's pictures were written, as asked.

Still stuck

Email livingfailuregames@gmail.com with your Unity version, the puzzle's seed and settings (the window's Copy button copies the seed), and the notes it shows. A seed rebuilds the puzzle exactly, so that is usually all it takes to see what you see.

About

About Simple Match

Simple Match is a match-3 puzzle generator for Unity, made by Living Failure. It builds puzzles to clear the board, collect pieces, peel layers off or bring exit pieces down, with bonus pieces and combos, crates, fixed pieces, type filters, spreading moss, countdowns, conveyor belts, and refill levels where new pieces drop in from a seeded stream. Then it solves each puzzle exactly, stamps it with its proven par and a 1 to 5 star difficulty, and stores it as a ScriptableObject or exports it as a picture. A complete 3D demo with 48 levels, built entirely in code, is included, and it can tell the player when a level can't be won any more.

Design principles

  • Solve exactly. The par, the number of best solutions and the odds come from searching every position a puzzle can reach, not from an estimate. A search that runs out of budget is never passed off as proven.
  • Measure, then promise. The star bands, the presets, Auto's fixes, every gimmick's placement rule and the demo's difficulty recipes all come from measuring real puzzles, hundreds at a time, and the docs say so where it matters.
  • Be honest. When a request can't be met, the puzzle's notes say so. Nothing is quietly downgraded, and the store page claims nothing the solver can't back up.
  • Every gimmick matters. A crate, filter, belt or countdown goes only where the puzzle really uses it and its win still works. Gimmicks that measured as doing nothing, or as handing out shortcuts, were left out of the generator (portals and gravity are in the rules, for you to place by hand).
  • One set of rules. The generator, the solver and the play rules your game uses all resolve a swap the same way, wave by wave, so your game and the par always agree.
  • Not by colour alone. Every piece type and every gimmick has its own shape as well as its own colour, in the pictures and in the demo, so colour-blind players can play every puzzle.
  • No bloat. The core has no package dependencies, and the demo and the editor tools live in their own assemblies. The demo needs no prefabs, models, textures or audio files.
