Reading the notes

Whenever the generator can't give you exactly what you asked for, it writes a note on the puzzle instead of quietly giving you less. The analysis pane shows every note, and so does MatchData.Notes from code. MatchAudit.MeetsRequest(puzzle) tells you whether a puzzle got everything its settings asked for and MatchAudit.Missing(puzzle) lists what it missed. In the window, Only on target hides the puzzles that missed something, and Save > Pack (on target only) leaves them out of a pack.

"No puzzle met the settings in 20 tries (...): this is the closest"

No seed gave a puzzle that met every hard requirement: solvable, the par proven and inside your par window, on your star target, with the bonus pieces needed if you asked for that. The note lists what it was looking for. Try, in this order:

  • Auto, which fixes settings that fight each other and says what it changed;
  • more Max attempts under Advanced, so more seeds are tried;
  • a wider par window, or a board that can hold the par you want (a built puzzle's par is at most its Lines minus 1);
  • one star more or less (see below).

The stars aren't the ones asked for

The stars come from how often a player swapping at random (on refill levels, a half-greedy player) wins within the move limit. Some settings can't reach every star:

  • Small boards make easy puzzles. Measured on the presets: 5 x 5 with 3 shapes rates 1 to 3 stars (mostly 2), 6 x 6 with 4 shapes 1 to 4, and 7 x 7 with 4 shapes mostly 4 to 5. For 4 or 5 stars use a bigger board, more lines or more shapes, or a gimmick that makes puzzles harder (spreading, countdowns with no slack, belts).
  • Moves to spare make a puzzle easier, so its stars drop: on collect goals and refill levels this setting decides much of the difficulty.
  • Collect on bonus boards is easy once bonus pieces fly: it rates up to about 4 stars.
  • Fixed pieces and type filters raise the par but can make a random player win slightly more often (fewer pieces to swap wrongly), so they can lower the stars.

Fewer gimmicks than asked

Every gimmick is placed only where the known win still works, and kept only when it changes the puzzle. When there is no such place, the puzzle gets fewer (or none) and a note says why, for example "Breakables: no cell that stays empty is next to a match of this puzzle's swaps, so it has none. A taller board leaves more room above the pieces". What helps:

  • Crates, moss, exit pieces need empty room above the pieces: a taller board, fewer lines, or Fit off. They are for clear-all puzzles without bonus pieces (exit pieces also for ExitAll); a dealt bonus board is full, so it has none.
  • Spreading keeps 3 to 4 cells at most; GenerateVerified tries more seeds when a puzzle got none.
  • Refill levels switch off crates, fixed pieces, filters, moss, countdowns, exit pieces and belts.

"The solver's state budget ran out"

The solver searched as many positions as State budget (under Advanced) allows without finishing. The generator never accepts such a puzzle as proven; it tries another seed. Raise the budget, or make the puzzle smaller: fewer lines (up to 12 are proven in under 0.3 s), fewer shapes on a bonus board, or a smaller board.

Generation feels slow

Measured times for one puzzle, proof and odds included: built puzzles up to 12 lines well under a second, 13 to 15 lines 1 to 10 s; bonus boards 6 x 6 up to about 1 s, 7 x 7 with 5 shapes 1 to 3 s, 7 x 7 with 4 shapes about 14 s; refill levels mostly a few seconds, large or hard ones 10 to 30 s. A star target multiplies that by the seeds it takes to hit.

Generation runs on a background thread, so the editor stays usable. Set Time limit (s) and a puzzle that takes longer is listed as not made, and Cancel stops at once. For bonus boards, 5 shapes are faster than 4 (more shapes, fewer matches to search).

The warning says nothing on a refill level

The dead-end warning asks MatchSolver.Outlook, which searches up to MatchGame.OutlookBudget positions (200,000), then once more ten times as far. On a big refill level it can still run out and answer Unknown, and the demo then says nothing rather than guess. It is never wrong, only sometimes silent. Raise the budget for more answers at the cost of more time on a worker thread.

A hand-made board is refused

MatchRules.Validate(puzzle, out error) says why in words. The usual causes:

  • a piece floating over an empty cell (every piece must rest on the bottom, a blocked cell, a crate or another piece);
  • a line of three already on the board at the start;
  • an array of the wrong length (each gimmick array has one entry per cell), or a type outside 0 to Types - 1;
  • row 0 is the bottom row: a board typed top row first comes out upside down.

Then MatchAudit.Verify solves it and stamps its par, odds and stars.

The demo ignores taps or keys

  • The demo reads the Input System package: set Project Settings > Player > Active Input Handling to Input System Package or Both.
  • The HUD and hub make an EventSystem with the Input System's UI module when the scene has none. If your scene has one with the old StandaloneInputModule, swap it for InputSystemUIInputModule.
  • Taps are ignored while a move is still playing out and over the HUD's buttons.

Pink or dark in a pipeline

  • Pink in a build: the demo makes its materials in code, and a build strips shaders that only code uses. Before every build, MatchLookKeeper writes Runtime/Demo/Resources/SimpleMatchLook.mat with the active pipeline's lit shader, so the build keeps it. If you see pink, check that file exists after a build (deleting it is fine: the next build writes it again).
  • Dark or tinted in HDRP: out of the box HDRP uses physical light units, automatic exposure and sky lighting. MatchDemo.HdrpLook (on by default) adds a global volume at priority 10 with a fixed exposure and a gradient sky, and sets the sun in lux, so the demo looks the same as on the other pipelines. Turn it off to let your own volumes and lights take over.

Resetting the demo's progress

The demo saves each pack level's best moves in PlayerPrefs, under SimpleMatch.Demo.Best.<pack name>.<level>, and the warning toggle under SimpleMatch.Demo.WarnDeadEnds. Call MatchPackProgress.Clear(packName, count) to forget a pack, or delete those keys. A pack's progress is filed under its pack name, so renaming a pack starts it fresh.

Seeds and versions

The same settings and seed always give the same puzzle within a version of Simple Match. A future version that improves the generator may change what a seed produces. Saved assets store the puzzle itself, not just its seed, so your saved puzzles and packs never change. Verified generation stores the seed that actually worked, such as level-1#2, so copying a puzzle's own seed always rebuilds that exact puzzle. Refill levels store their stream's seed too, so they drop the same pieces every time.

Console messages

  • "[Simple Match] Documentation page not found": the documentation menu looks for these pages in a folder whose path contains SimpleMatch and Documentation. Keep the Documentation folder inside the Simple Match folder.
  • "[Simple Match] Generation failed": something went wrong on the window's generation thread. Please send the message with your settings and seed.
  • "[Simple Match] Kept ... in the build for the demo": the build step wrote the look material, which is expected after you switch render pipelines.
  • "[Simple Match] No lit shader found for the active render pipeline": the pipeline asset has no default material, so the demo may draw pink in a build. Give the pipeline asset a default material.
  • "[Simple Match] Exported 12 files from ...": a pack's pictures were written, as asked.

Still stuck

Email livingfailuregames@gmail.com with your Unity version, the puzzle's seed and settings (the window's Copy button copies the seed), and the notes it shows. A seed rebuilds the puzzle exactly, so that is usually all it takes to see what you see.
