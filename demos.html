The demo is a small, complete match-3 game built on the asset, so you can play what the generator makes and take whatever you need from it. Everything in it is made in code: no prefabs, models, textures or audio files.

The demo scene

Open Assets/SimpleMatch/Scenes/SimpleMatchDemo.unity and press Play. The scene holds a camera, a light and one MatchDemo component with the four level packs in its slots. It needs the Input System package (Active Input Handling set to Input System Package or Both) and Unity UI.

One component: MatchDemo

Drop MatchDemo in any scene with a camera and press Play. It builds the board and the game, the HUD, the hub, the sounds and the effects, and lights the scene if it has no light. Its fields:

FieldWhat it does
CameraThe camera that shows the board (empty = the main camera).
UiThroughCameraDraw the HUD and the hub through the camera (Screen Space - Camera) instead of over the screen.
PitchHow steeply the camera looks down to start with.
HdrpLookHDRP only: a small volume (fixed exposure, a gradient sky) so it looks as it does in the other pipelines.
ClearPack, BonusPack, GimmicksPack, RefillPackThe level packs (MatchPuzzlePack) of the four modes; empty = that mode's Level Pack tab says so.

The flow

A welcome panel says what Simple Match is; Let's play goes on to four mode cards, each with a picture of a real puzzle of its mode. A card opens its setup:

  • Endless: pick the kind and the difficulty (1 to 5 stars) and press Play. A brand new puzzle is made to order on a background thread (on WebGL, which has no threads, straight away). New puzzle on the win panel makes another to the same order.
  • Level Pack: the mode's levels as numbered buttons, grey to do, the mode's colour solved, gold solved on par. Play starts the first level not yet solved. The best moves per level are kept (in PlayerPrefs: MatchPackProgress), and the win panel says "Level complete!" or how you did against your best. Next level goes on.

Menu or Esc goes back. The hub's Warnings button (or W in play) turns the dead-end warning on or off, remembered between sessions.

The four modes

ModeKinds (Endless)
ClearSquare or shaped (a diamond, heart or ring) boards: clear every piece.
Bonus PiecesClear the board or collect, with bonus pieces (collect goes up to 4 stars).
GimmicksLayers, crates, fixed pieces, filters, moss, countdowns, exits or belts.
RefillClassic match-3 with new pieces dropping in: collect, clear layers, or bring down the exit pieces that drop in.

The recipes behind each kind and star count are in MatchEndless.Settings, pure C#, and were measured on 8 deals each: 72 of 74 land on their stars every time, and the other two (refill exit drops at 3 and 4 stars) land one star off in one deal of eight. Most puzzles are made in under 3 seconds; the slowest, refill exit drops and layers at 4 stars, can take 10 to 30.

The level packs

Four packs in Assets/SimpleMatch/Generated, 48 levels in all: Clear 12, Bonus Pieces 10, Gimmicks 16 (each gimmick twice, gently and then hard) and Refill 10. Each climbs from 1 star to 5, so nobody starts on the hard ones. They were made from fixed seeds with the same Endless recipes, and every level was checked: valid, re-verified unchanged, on its exact stars, everything asked for in it, and played through the game to par.

Make your own in the Match Generator window (Save > Pack (all)) and drop it in a pack slot.

Playing

Tap a piece (it lifts over a white ring) and then a neighbour, or drag a piece onto a neighbour, to swap. A swap that makes no match slides over and back; a piece that can't move wiggles. Every step of a move is shown: the swap, the matches popping, pieces falling along their real paths (sideways with gravity, through portals), new pieces sliding in, belts moving, a reshuffle lifting every moved piece to its own height so none passes through another. After each step the board is put exactly where the rules say, so it never drifts.

KeyDoes

The HUD shows the goal in words, how far along it is, the board, the difficulty, moves against the limit, and a status line for a win, a loss, a refused swap's reason, or the dead-end warning ("This can't be won any more: it needs more than the 3 moves left. Undo or restart."). That warning comes from asking MatchSolver.Outlook after every move, on a background thread, so play never waits for it.

Sounds and effects

MatchBeeper synthesizes every sound in code: a pop with a note for each match, one step up the chord for each wave of a cascade, a sparkle for a bonus piece made, a blast for each kind of bonus clear, a knock or a crack for a crate, a tick-tock for a countdown on its last move, and tunes for the win and the loss. MatchEffects throws small lit cubes for each moment (no particle shaders, so they look the same in every pipeline): bursts in a piece's colour, a streak along a line clear, splinters, confetti. Both just listen to the game's Cue and Moment events.

Render pipelines

The demo makes its materials from the active pipeline's default lit shader, so it works in URP, HDRP and the Built-in pipeline (checked in all three: every material on the pipeline's lit shader, none pink, the pictures the same). A shader that only code uses would be stripped from a build, so MatchLookKeeper writes Runtime/Demo/Resources/SimpleMatchLook.mat before every build, a plain material with that shader, so the build keeps it. It changes no project settings, and you can delete the material: the next build writes it again. On HDRP, DemoHdrp adds a small volume so it looks the same.

Using the pieces yourself

Each part works on its own:

ComponentWhat it gives you
MatchBoardViewBuilds any MatchData in 3D: Build(puzzle), Show(state) / Sync(state), CellWorld(cell), CellAt(point), FramePose for the camera.
MatchGamePlays it on that view: Begin(puzzle), Swap(a, b), Tap, Drag, Undo, Restart, ShowHint, StartAutoSolve; events Step, Moved, Won, Lost, NoWay, Cue, Moment; PointerInput / KeyboardInput off to drive it yourself, CameraControls on to let it frame the board.
MatchHud, MatchHubThe in-game HUD (with FreeAreas for the camera) and the front screen, built in code with uGUI.
MatchBeeper, MatchEffectsHook them to Cue and Moment.
MatchPieces, MatchGimmicksThe meshes of every piece and gimmick, if you want them in your own scene.
// a board and a game on any GameObject, no hub, no HUD
var go = new GameObject("Match");
go.AddComponent<MatchBoardView>();
var game = go.AddComponent<MatchGame>();
game.CameraControls = true;
game.Won += g => Debug.Log($"won in {g.Moves}");
game.Lost += why => Debug.Log("lost: " + why);
game.Begin(puzzle);
